It's ok to admit that you feel overwhelmed, especially nowadays in the age of ai. Everything is happening too fast and it is somehow expected of you in the tech industry to keep up.

Fomo is a real thing, because for me it hasn't been the number of the new things that has been the problem. It's the excitement. The urge of trying new things, testing out the next big thing, catching up on my learning backlog with ai and waiting for the next prompt and then next and then next.

[The syntax yt video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPUn1Fnfn0k) came at the perfect time and i needed to hear this. I was feeling the same way without knowing it and the rush of excitement had me blind to it. From now on, i will try to keep my excitement in a jar and take a healthy dose of it from time to time. But i'm not sure if the industry feels the same. At least the charts are telling the same story about devs. You should really check them out [https://ai-health.syntax.fm/](https://ai-health.syntax.fm/)

[![Chart from ai-health.syntax.fm](/images/blog/dev-health-ai-aug19.png)](ai-health.syntax.fm)

How should we balance it all? well I can talk only about myself. Previously i talk about [saying no more often](https://sinabastani.dev/blog/slow-the-fuck-down) but i didn't say no to myself. So i need to practice that more and try to organize my time in a way that I can work on my learning and skills in a healthy pace even if it is easier than ever nowadays.

Also It is important to filter out the hype and buzzwords and just focus on the things that you enjoy. Recently i have been living in the terminal more than usual so i really wanted to  make a TUI on my own! I started a [small project](https://github.com/sina96/ytunes) myself and have been enjoying focusing on learning golang, cicd workflows and how to actually manage an open source project. I would recommend doing so rather than following unnecessary AI slop setups.
